# DIPS Ojos Project: Characterizing Anomalies in Exoplanet Transits

The **DIPS Ojos (Detecting Irregular Photometric Signals Ojímetro – Survey)** project is established as a pro-am collaborative project between researchers at the [University Institute of Space Sciences and Technologies of Asturias (ICTEA)](https://ictea.uniovi.es/) – [Universidad de Oviedo](https://www.uniovi.es/) and amateur astronomers from the [Asturian Astronomical Society Omega](https://saaomega.es/). It is dedicated to the visual identification of morphological irregularities in exoplanet transits present in high-precision light curves.

Although in the current era it is indispensable to have automatic tools to analyze the immense datasets available, the core of our research lies in the premise that the human eye, properly trained, possesses a unique capacity to discern complex patterns and subtle signals hidden within noise, which often elude automatic detection algorithms.

Through this collaborative effort, we seek to detect and catalog anomalous events that allow us to deepen our understanding of the architecture of stellar systems and their planets.

## Scientific Objectives

The analysis in **DIPS Ojos** focuses on detecting subtle signals that may be present in exoplanet transits, with special emphasis on the detection of:

- **Starspot Crossing Events:** slight flux increases during exoplanet transits that reveal the position and size of active regions on the stellar surface, fundamental for modeling the rotation and magnetism of the star crossed by the exoplanet.

![](/docs/about/2.png)

*Real Starspot Crossing Event in a transit of planet Kepler-289c*

- **Transit Timing Variations (TTVs):** Identification of deviations in event duration that may suggest dynamic interactions within the system or a revision and refinement of planetary parameters currently considered acceptable.
- **Transient and Exotic Events:** We track the presence of signals compatible with stellar flares occurring during transits, circumplanetary dust structures, ring systems, mutual phenomena between multiple transiting planets or even satellites. All phenomena that require detailed visual inspection to be differentiated from instrumental artifacts.

## Methodology

In this first pilot phase, where we are testing the detection efficiency of the collaboration, a group of ten amateur astronomers has visually inspected 897 transits corresponding to 140 light curves of 51 real exoplanets observed by the Kepler space mission, along with another 100 simulated light curves in which Starspot Crossing Event or exomoon signals have been injected. Superimposed on the transit (real or simulated) is the model that it ideally should have, so that these signals are visually identified as deviations from the theoretical model.

![](/docs/about/4.png)

*Simulated transit with a Starspot Crossing Event (left) and transit of planet Kepler-89d (right) along with their superimposed theoretical models.*

To ensure that findings in real data have a solid statistical basis, collaborators periodically analyze simulated curves where synthetic signals with known parameters have been injected. These simulated curves allow us to establish an efficiency level for each project participant and enable prioritizing the most promising candidates for subsequent analysis by professional researchers.

## Platform Access and Tools

We invite the scientific community and interested observers to explore the **DIPS Ojos** diagnostic tool. The interface has been designed to facilitate the marking of six key features, allowing detailed classification of each transit and any anomalies that may be present in it.

Public access (in test mode) has been enabled for the classification tool. You can experiment by visualizing and classifying the available curves. To preserve data integrity, classifications made in this mode are not stored in the database.
